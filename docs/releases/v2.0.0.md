**In-Season mode.** Release 1 was a draft board. Release 2 keeps that board unchanged and adds a
second product beside it: a rest-of-season view that estimates what is *left* of the season from
the season's own evidence, and an opportunity board that puts that value beside what the fantasy
market is actually doing with players right now.

The two are deliberately not merged. A preseason fair rank and a rest-of-season fair rank come
from different models, over different horizons, against different replacement baselines. They may
sit side by side; they may never be averaged into one "true" rank. Every column heading, every
export column and every player card in this release is built around that distinction.

## What shipped

- **A rest-of-season model in production.** `intrinsic-ros-v1` — the availability ×
  conditional-performance hurdle Phase 11 evaluated chronologically and promoted under
  `ros_promotion_v2` — now exists as a committed, versioned artifact fitted on 2017–2025 and
  serving 2026. It is the *same* model: a configuration hash over its architecture, parameters,
  calibration, composition rule and seed material is recorded on the artifact and asserted at
  load, so "we only refitted it" is checkable rather than claimed (ADR-078).
- **Deterministic season-state switching.** `season_state_v1` derives the product mode from the
  NFL schedule — never from a date literal — and resolves the same way for the same schedule and
  clock. Before the season's first regular-season kickoff the site is the draft board; from
  kickoff it opens on the rest-of-season board. The draft board stays reachable all season, in
  the URL, with the same scoring and team controls.
- **A rest-of-season build with its own freshness gate.** `ros_source_freshness_v1` refuses to
  build `through_week=N` until upstream carries every scheduled team for weeks 1..N with no gap
  behind N. *Played* and *available* are two facts and are decided by two rules.
- **The ROS Tier Board.** ROS fair rank, ROS position rank, expected ROS VORP with a P25–P75
  interval, expected remaining points and games, ROS tier, uncertainty, the change against the
  preseason rank, and a current-status annotation kept visibly and structurally apart from every
  model input.
- **The long-absence disclosure contract, implemented exactly** (ADR-076): a machine-readable
  flag, `weeks_since_last_game` beside it, an explicit statement that the estimate uses **no**
  injury or practice-report information, a definition in observable terms only ("has not appeared
  for N weeks"), the measured ordering weakness inside that cohort, and three channels on the
  badge so the flag is never carried by colour alone.
- **The Opportunity Board.** Sleeper add/drop counts over a named, retained window beside
  rest-of-season value. Counts are counts: never called ADP, never differenced against a rank,
  never blended into a score. Three orderings, no composite. A player from beyond the tier depth
  can be surfaced by current evidence and is marked as surfaced, with no tier.
- **A behaviour capture that cannot move a number.** Add/drop snapshots land in the same
  append-only, content-hashed store as market and status history, under a `behavior/` prefix.
  The opportunity artifact copies its intrinsic columns from the ROS artifact verbatim, and a
  cross-artifact check compares seven of them over the **published bytes** of both files.
- **Operations that fail closed.** The full Sleeper player map is fetched at most once a day;
  add/drop runs on the documented in-season cadence. A behaviour-source failure is a warning that
  empties three columns. A critical ROS input failure publishes **nothing** — the whole bundle is
  staged and swapped atomically — and the previous deployment stands. Draft and ROS artifacts
  validate independently.

### Clause 1: what shipped, and what did not

**FantasyPros is not published, and cannot be from the provisioned key.** Phase 10 built the
adapter, the budget, the identity linkage and the capture, then measured what the key returns
(ADR-064): every response carries `public_api_limited: true` and `tier: "free"`, ten rows per
call, **40 distinct players** across QB/RB/WR/TE against a documented `count` of 407 receivers
alone. There is no ADP endpoint at all — `/adp` returns `403 Missing Authentication Token`.

The original criterion is recorded as **historically unmet**. It is not restated, softened, or
marked passed. What ships instead is stated positively: **FFC and MyFantasyLeague for draft ADP**,
published as independent comparisons and never conflated, with **Sleeper add/drop** for in-season
opportunity. FantasyPros stays `benchmark_only` and feeds nothing — no public comparison, no
model, no surface decision — with an exact, tested revisit condition: a key whose responses omit
`public_api_limited` and whose `count` equals the rows delivered flips `MarketSourceSpec.
publishable`, one line.

**ADR-080 removes this from the blocker list.** A blocker is something the project can act on; a
vendor entitlement tier is not, and holding a finished release against one converts a documented
limitation into an indefinite hold. Shipping a 40-player consensus as though it were a market
would be strictly worse than shipping none. If the entitlement changes, publishing FantasyPros is
an ordinary Phase-13 source change, not a re-opening of Release 2.

### The one thing still open

**No live in-season week has ever been built.** Today is 2026-09-04; the first 2026 regular-season
kickoff is `2026-09-10T00:20:00Z`, so `season-state` resolves to `preseason_draft` and the
in-season path cannot run against real 2026 data before the release. Everything in-season is
proven on fixtures, on unit and end-to-end tests, and on the 2024 rehearsal — which is stronger
than a plan and weaker than an observation. **The first post-Week-1 refresh (`"40 12 * * 2"`) must
be watched, not assumed.** Until it runs, the in-season product is validated but unobserved, and
this is the one thing about Release 2 a reader should hold loosely.

This stays open because it is an observation the project will make, not an entitlement it cannot
obtain. It is an operational item for the days after merge, not a reason to hold the release.

### The season's two edges, fixed before release (ADR-079)

A pre-merge review found three lifecycle defects that fixtures and a mid-season rehearsal could
not reach, because both sit in the middle of a season rather than at its edges:

1. **Opening week would have frozen the site.** `season_state_v1` flips the mode at the first
   kickoff; `ros_cutoff_v1` refuses week 0. Between them is a window of several days in which the
   mode says In-Season and no board can be built — and the refresh ran `build-ros` on the *mode*,
   hit a critical, failed the build job, and took the **draft** deploy down with it, because the
   deploy job is downstream. The ROS build is now gated on `latest_snapshot_week`, the freshness
   gate distinguishes the opening-week cadence (a warning) from a real outage (still critical),
   and the packaging step keys on what the build actually produced.
2. **The end of the season would have crash-looped.** At `season_complete` the deepest available
   week is the last *scored* week, which `RosCutoff` refuses by raising. Nothing caught it. The
   cutoff is now bounded by the remaining horizon before the constructor sees it, and the refusal
   is a warning: the last published board is the final one, and no board of structural zeros is
   published to keep a tab populated.
3. **The freshness gate compared counts, not clubs.** It documented "every team the schedule says
   played appears in the weekly statistics" and implemented `games * 2` against a distinct count.
   A week that lost one club and gained a stray row for another passed. Both sides now go through
   `normalize_team_code` and the comparison is a set comparison, with the missing and unexpected
   clubs named in the diagnostic.

The product gained a third situation as a result — *season under way, no board yet* — which the
draft build's own `build_metadata.season_state` block makes sayable, and which the page states in
the build's words rather than in the frontend's. Four lifecycle states plus the two at the far end
have a test each (`tests/unit/test_ros_lifecycle.py`), the workflow's gate is pinned as a test,
and both new product states have a screenshot.

## Known Release 2 limitations

Measured, published, and deliberately not repaired by moving a threshold. The six Phase-11
limitations are inherited unchanged and are on the model card in full; the ones a reader meets on
the screen are:

- **Rest-of-season tiers are bands, not lines.** Both the convergence and the tier-stability gates
  fail, and the board says so where the tiers are drawn (ADR-074). Membership reproduces across
  resamples; exact cut positions do not.
- **10,000 draws is a declared fallback**, not a converged count. It is printed as such.
- **The model has no injury information of any kind.** It infers absence from appearances alone.
  Inside the long-absence cohort its ordering is close to random — Spearman 0.311 against 0.797 on
  the full universe — and that number is on the board, not only in a document (ADR-076).
- **It is overconfident on high-draft-capital rookies** — coverage 0.763 against an attainable
  0.898, the tightest clause in the promotion gate.
- **Add/drop counts are counts.** They are not a price, not a rank, and are never subtracted from
  one. The source publishes no observation time of its own, so the timestamp shown is when the
  snapshot was retrieved.
- **Behaviour decides visibility, never value.** A surfaced player is published because current
  evidence made him relevant; nothing about that changes his rest-of-season number.

## Not in Release 2, and deliberately so

Start/sit rankings, lineup optimisation, trade evaluation, roster import, a learned opportunity
score, and any injury model. Each was out of scope by instruction and none is half-built. The
learned arbitrage model still waits on point-in-time historical market coverage, as in V1.
